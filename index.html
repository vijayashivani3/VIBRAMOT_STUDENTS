How it works

From motor to diagnosis in four steps

Place a motor on the test rig

Choose a 3-phase induction, DC shunt, synchronous or BLDC motor. It is mounted on a virtual rig with an accelerometer on the housing, like the real setup.

Set speed, fault and severity

Pick a healthy motor or inject imbalance, misalignment, or an outer-race, inner-race or rolling-element bearing defect.

  • Shaft speed 300 to 3600 RPM
  • Fault severity and noise floor
  • Bearing geometry: balls N, d/D ratio, contact angle

Run the analysis

The simulator generates the accelerometer signal at 4000 Hz, applies a Hann window and computes a 2048-point radix-2 FFT, all in your browser.

Read the result

The status shows Normal or the detected fault. The readout lists shaft frequency, expected fault frequency, peak amplitude and threshold so you can see why.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need any hardware or software?

No. VIBRAMOT runs in a modern browser with an internet connection. Nothing is installed.

Where do the signals come from?

Signals are generated from the standard fault-frequency formulas, so every peak can be checked by hand and every run is repeatable. Students learn the diagnostic method here and carry it to real machines.

Does the motor type change the signal?

Motor type sets the rig and label. All motors share one vibration model, so faults can be compared on equal terms across machines.

Which faults can it diagnose?

Rotor imbalance, shaft misalignment, and bearing outer-race, inner-race and rolling-element defects, one fault at a time.

Fault signatures and the signal chain
Each fault leaves a fingerprint at a predictable frequency. Shaft frequency is fr = RPM / 60.


FaultWhere it appearsFormula


Imbalance1× shaft frequencyf = fr
Misalignment2× shaft frequency (real machines add harmonics)f = 2fr


Outer race (BPFO)Ball-pass frequency, outer race(N/2)·fr·[1 − (d/D)cosθ]


Inner race (BPFI)Ball-pass frequency, inner race(N/2)·fr·[1 + (d/D)cosθ]


Rolling element (BSF)Ball spin frequency(D/2d)·fr·[1 − (d/D)²cos²θ]


N = rolling elements, d = ball diameter, D = pitch diameter, θ = contact angle.


What each fault looks like, and what to do
FaultIn the motor viewUsual causeRecommended action
ImbalanceRed mass on the rotor; motor shakes in a circle each turnDirt build-up, lost balance weight, bent shaftBalance the rotor in place
MisalignmentLoad shaft tilts and offsets at the couplingSoft foot, thermal growth, poor installationRealign with a dial indicator or laser tool
Outer-race defectPit on the fixed ring flashes as each ball passesContamination, poor lubrication, overloadTrend the peak and plan a bearing change
Inner-race defectPit on the spinning ring flashes as each ball passesFatigue, shaft fit problems, lubrication lossPlan bearing replacement
Rolling-element defectMarked ball flashes as it touches each ringDebris damage, corrosion, lubricant breakdownReplace the bearing at the next stop


Ideas the dashboard makes visible


Nyquist sampling
Sample at least twice the highest frequency of interest (fs ≥ 2fmax) or the spectrum aliases. The default fs is 4000 Hz; change it on the dashboard to see aliasing.


Windowing
A Hann window tapers the edges so spectral leakage does not smear the fault peak.


FFT
Converts the time signal to frequency; a peak at the expected fault frequency indicates the fault.


Threshold detection
The detector scans every candidate fault frequency and compares each peak with a robust noise floor (median + 10·MAD). The strongest candidate above threshold is reported.
Evaluation
How well does the detector perform?


A Monte Carlo test of the blind detector on synthetic signals. Each trial uses fresh noise; shaft speed cycles through 1200–3000 RPM; bearing N = 9, d/D = 0.30, θ = 0°; fs = 4000 Hz, Hann window. Fault amplitude is 1.5 × severity relative to the rotation component.




 


How to read it


  • Detected: at least one candidate frequency exceeds its threshold.
  • Identified: the top-ranked candidate is the injected fault.
  • False alarm: a healthy motor is flagged as faulty.


Findings


  • No false alarms in 1000 healthy trials at each of the three noise levels.
    • 

  • Misalignment and the three bearing faults are detected and correctly identified at 98–100% from severity 0.05 at noise 0.3, and from severity 0.1 at noise 0.6. Whenever a fault was detected it was identified correctly.
    • 

  • Imbalance adds to the 1× component a healthy machine already has, and is detected once it raises that amplitude by about 25% (severity of about 0.2 or more).


Scope of this evaluation
  • The test uses the VIBRAMOT signal generator with white noise and one fault at a time, which makes every result repeatable and easy to check.
  • Shaft speeds from 1200 to 3000 rpm are covered (fs = 4000 Hz, 2048 samples, resolution 1.95 Hz), where all five fault peaks are resolved from the 1× line.
  • The adaptive threshold (spectrum median + 10 robust standard deviations) gave zero false alarms across 3000 healthy trials.

For lab in-charges
What to record about each motor


Motor nameplate details
Type, rated power (kW/HP), rated speed (RPM), supply and number of poles. Rated speed sets the shaft frequency fr = RPM / 60 used by the simulator.


Bearing designation and geometry
Note the bearing number (for example 6205) and look up the number of balls N, ball diameter d and pitch diameter D from the datasheet. The commonly cited CWRU test bearing is a 6205 with 9 balls, d ≈ 7.94 mm, D ≈ 39.04 mm (d/D ≈ 0.20); verify against your own datasheet. These feed the bearing-geometry sliders.


Quantity, condition and availability
How many units exist, whether any are known to be faulty, and which sessions use them. This tells departments where a virtual practice session helps most.


Existing vibration instruments
Whether an accelerometer, DAQ or analyser exists. If not, VIBRAMOT is the only way students see a spectrum.

About the project
About us
Making vibration diagnosis learnable for every student


VIBRAMOT exists for one reason: students who cannot reach a motor lab should still learn how faults are found.


The problem
Real vibration analysis needs accelerometers, motor rigs and analysers. Free IoT simulators do not model vibration signals, free spectrum viewers lack fault injection, and commercial tools are paid.


Our approach
Simulate the whole chain in the browser: machine, vibration, windowing, FFT, fault frequencies, decision. Free, installation-free and explainable.


Our principle
A transparent teaching tool. Every diagnosis shows its numbers: expected frequency, measured peak and threshold, and the maths is verified.


Under the hood
StageImplementation


Signal generatorShaft tone + fault tone + random noise, sampled at 4000 Hz (Nyquist limit 2000 Hz)


WindowingHann window, 2048 samples
FFTIterative radix-2 Cooley–Tukey, written from scratch in JavaScript


DetectionBlind scan of five candidate frequencies (±2 bins) against a median + 10·MAD noise floor; imbalance is compared with the healthy 1× reference


InterfaceHTML, CSS, JavaScript and Chart.js; runs fully client-side

How we checked it


FFT vs numpy
Identical input gave outputs matching to about 5.5×10⁻¹⁵, with the peak at bin 40 as predicted.


Fault frequencies
For 1500 RPM, N = 9, d/D = 0.30, the BPFO is 78.75 Hz and the spectrum peak lands on it.


Scope
Model-generated signals with one fault at a time, evaluated on simulated data: an ideal setting for learning the principles of diagnosis.
